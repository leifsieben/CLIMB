## SI · Wikipedia through the SMILES tokenizer: is the benefit chemistry-specific?

A ModernBERT pretrained on **English Wikipedia** — zero chemistry — but tokenized with the *SMILES*
BPE and length-matched to the SMILES corpus (chunk lengths sampled from the real SMILES length
distribution), then frozen-probed exactly like the other arms (native 5-fold scaffold CV, 3 seeds).
Bars are lift over the random-init frozen floor; `real` is the SMILES-pretrained `unsup_8M`.

Wikipedia beats no_pretrain on 6/7 tasks and **matches real SMILES on QM7** (both 199.4 vs floor
205.3). So a large part of the MLM benefit is **domain-general**, not chemistry-specific.

Two guards rule out the obvious confounds:

- **Coverage** (`wiki_coverage.json`): Wikipedia trained **96.9%** of eval-token *mass* ≥1×
  (**88.6%** ≥1000×), so this is not an undertrained-embedding artifact. QM7 has the *lowest* coverage
  (85% ≥1×) yet transfers *fully* — which strengthens the result.
- **Corpus divergence** (`wiki_vs_smiles_stats.json`): same tokenizer, matched lengths (median 37
  both), but the token **marginals are near-maximally divergent** (JS = 0.93 of 1.0 bits); 435
  chemistry-only tokens (incl. stereo `@@`/`@`) go unfilled. Transfer happens *despite* an orthogonal
  token marginal — consistent with Fig SA (it is not the corpus marginal). HIV is scored by ROC-AUC here.
